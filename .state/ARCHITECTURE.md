## Design patterns

- Decision story = reasoning process; scroll UI is the interface
- Approach B manifests; allow-listed visuals only
- react-scrollama + CSS sticky; one path all breakpoints
- **Continuous spine:** `TransmissionSpine` + `BufferMarkField` — acts change zoom/filter, not metaphor
- **Directed film:** one canvas, scroll-scrubbed camera, loan dots from `buildField`. This is the reference story people open. The essay manifest stays on the engine and is not linked from the landing or the film.
- **No dead air:** a scrubbed film is a pure function of scroll, so it freezes exactly when the reader is reading. Held beats creep the camera and keep the marks living; `e2e/dead-air.spec.ts` measures it in changed pixels rather than asserting it.
- Extract grammar from real stories; no speculative template farm
- AI in production pipeline later — not the brand headline
- Evidence: notebooks/MCP → freeze JSON → manifest display (never invent at render)
- **Narration reads the pack, not the prose.** Story 3's copy (`src/components/film/recovery-copy.ts`) formats every figure out of the frozen JSON, so a re-freeze moves the sentences with the data. Typing a number into a paragraph is how a film ends up contradicting its own evidence pack — which is exactly the class of drift that produced the "78–84%" error.
- **Interpolate the marks, snap the numbers.** A scrub moves continuously, but a counterfactual pack holds a handful of frozen variants. Marks may sit between two of them; a figure printed on screen snaps to the nearest frozen variant (`recoveryVariantIndex`). The film must never display a number it did not freeze.
- **A sampled rule is not an optimiser.** Where a counterfactual applies one allocation rule at a few strengths, the strongest available phrase is "best of the frozen variants". Story 3's rule is non-monotone on 6 of 7 lines, so the last strength is usually *not* the best one, and a test forbids the word "optimal" in the narration.
- **One stage, one camera.** A film's creep is applied once, as a transform on the stage, not threaded through a projection. Story 3 learned this the hard way: creep folded into the stop-axis projection moved the two panels sharing that axis and left the survival panel — on its own axis, a third of the frame — nailed down, which reads as a foreground drifting over a photograph. It also made a push in a horizontal stretch, because a span multiplier only narrows x. A panel on its own scale is the test: if the camera cannot move it, the camera is in the wrong place.
- **A quantity gets its own panel, never a second unnamed scale.** Story 3's decay curve is indexed by *stops after a late arrival* and its marks by *position in the run*. Laying one over the other was two quantities on one axis with one of them unlabelled. Three stacked panels over a shared x, and a panel for anything that does not share it.
- **Batch marks by colour and quantised alpha.** A five-pixel mark pays more for its `beginPath`/`arc`/`fill` than for its pixels. Twelve alpha steps let thousands share a path. The consequence is explicit: overlapping sub-paths of one path fill as a *union*, so marks inside a step stop compositing over each other and a saturated band becomes one that shows its density.
- **Thin the backdrop with the stage; never the subject.** Where a field exists at a size set by one act's needs, the parts no act looks at closely scale down with the available area, and the part the camera is on never does. Story 3 keeps 44 runs on the selected line at every width and samples the other six lines on a phone — so every act from the fourth on is pixel-identical everywhere, and the cost falls on the opening shot, where it is stated rather than hidden.
- **One camera over every layer (v3).** The data canvas, the Rive canvas and the SVG annotations sit in one container with one `translate3d · scale` transform (origin 0 0). Shots are `{focus, zoom, screen}`; FOCUS / TRACK / PULLBACK are one blend between the wide shot and a shot rebuilt from the entity's anchor every frame. Craft creep is applied to the camera only (`fieldFrame` passes `hold: 0`). Do not put `will-change: transform` on the camera: Chrome then rasterises the layer once at 1× and stretches SVG and small canvases.
- **Identity in the map, geometry in the renderer.** `EntityMap` throws on `x/y/width/height/left/top/anchor` in state. Positions are asked for through `getAnchor(id)`, which each layer answers in viewport CSS px via `getBoundingClientRect()` (already includes the camera).
- **Trigger cues reconcile, never replay.** A self-clocked visual (a Rive state machine) is armed / played / settled. Behind the cue → reset; past it → fire only on a forward crossing while visible and motion is allowed, otherwise settle to the end state. Pure function of (from, to, state), so scrubbing cannot stack animations.
- **Size a transformed canvas from its layout box.** The Rive canvas runtime sizes its backing store from `getBoundingClientRect()`, i.e. *after* the camera and open scale. `RiveLayer` re-sizes it from `offsetWidth × pixelRatio` on load and on `play()`; the scene passes `pixelRatio = DPR × camera zoom`. The data canvas raises its backing store the same way when the camera pushes in (capped at 12 MP).
- **Switch states without jumping a spinning part.** A Rive state change restarts the new animation's keys, so a wheel keyed in two states snaps to the new state's angle. `grid.riv` nests three groups per wheel: *place* (faded by the mass layer), *spin* (one looping animation on its own layer, never re-keyed) and *lag* (0 until a trip, then the accumulated fall behind full speed). A held state loops the lag by one spoke-repeat (a quarter turn), which is invisible because the spokes are symmetric.
- **A threshold catches more than the thing it is for.** A fixed "100 mHz in 5 s" detector caught 33 falls; 19 were lost generators. The rest were schedule steps, held-then-jump readings, falls from a local peak, snap-backs and fault-driven swings at the meter. Classify by shape (depth from the pre-event level, time to nadir, share left 30 s later, steepest step), keep every event in the pack with its class, and look at the borderline traces before trusting the rule.
- **Check the archive's clock.** Fingrid's 10 Hz archive is Finnish local time while its API is UTC; a DST day with a missing hour is the cheap proof. Pair on UTC and carry both times.
- **An illustration draws its constants from the pack's tests.** `grid-evidence.test.ts` checks the model's and the illustration's floor, reference loss, light and typical hours and reserve volume against the published figures in the frozen pack, so neither can drift from the evidence.
- **Teach a term where it is first needed; put depth on its own page.** A film opens with a short orientation card that uses no term the reader has to learn. Each term is taught at its first use by an in-place term button, collected in a Terms drawer, and guarded by a test that fails on use-before-teaching. Data, schema, pipeline, model and limitations live on an unlisted `/stories/<slug>/method` page generated from the pack, so it moves with a re-freeze. Kind badges link to it. Story-agnostic (Layer 1); specified in `docs/decision-specs/grid-inertia-fast-reserve.md`.
- **Every reference story ships its reader kit, and the validator says so.** The kit started as the grid story's and was retrofitted to the three older films the same day, which is what showed it was Layer 1: each story only supplied words, terms and a pack. `role: "reference"` without `reader` + `method` fails `validate:stories`. The term check found jargon in the older films' title cards and early beats (PD, gate, appetite, out-of-time, TARGET, IRB) that had been there since they shipped.
- **Narration lives outside client components.** The term-order check has to read the words that render, from the server and from tests. Copy functions inside a `"use client"` film are unreachable there, so each film's copy is a plain module the film imports.
- **Count from unrounded data.** The grid pack's hourly series is rounded for size; counting "hours below 150 GWs" from it gave 581 against 584. Counts the film quotes are frozen in the pack from the unrounded means (`years.months`), never recomputed from a display series.
- **Bundle runtime assets from `src/`.** `rive.wasm` and `household.riv` are referenced with `new URL(..., import.meta.url)`, so they travel with `src/**` in the Orbit sync instead of depending on `public/`.
- **Pull back on the subject; on a phone, words and picture take turns.** `wideShotOn(subject)` in `camera.ts` is the PULLBACK target when the subject is off-centre (the grid film's hour swung off a laptop screen with `wideShot`). On a phone the grid film maps track progress through `phoneMomentAt`: each beat's narration rises on a solid card while the film holds at the beat's start, then lowers to a kicker-and-label strip while the beat plays with the picture at full size. The phone track is `1300vh × PHONE_TRACK_SCALE` (CSS variable, so SSR and client agree); `window.__gridFilm.trackAt(film)` / `readAt(beat)` let tests address film moments and reading spans on either layout. Replaced the same-day attempt that shrank the phone picture to fit the tallest narration (reader: "we should not sacrifice on the graph"). See `docs/STORY_GRAMMAR.md` *Craft earned from the grid film*.
- **Review the picture, not the gate.** Every drawing bug in story 3 passed every test: unlabelled axes, marks off-plot, axis labels outside the clip, a split with nothing to split. `scripts/shoot-recovery.mjs` puts a frame per beat on disk. A gate measures the frames someone thought to point it at.

| 2026-09-26 | Evidence packs covering a year must be timetable-period scoped | Finnish timetables are re-cut annually: week over week an ordinary day moves 1.3% of legs by half a minute, and 2025-12-14 moved **18.8%**, with individual legs shifting up to 8.5 min. A median scheduled run time pooled over twelve months describes no timetable that was ever operated, and pooling it visibly diluted the mechanism (long-distance ρ −0.48 pooled versus −0.69 period-scoped). The freeze detects periods at max(10%, 3× the weekly baseline) and scopes every margin figure inside one, while survival and seasonality use the whole year. First threshold attempt at a flat 6% sat inside the noise and invented seven spurious periods — derive the threshold from the data. |
| 2026-09-26 | The unit of a schedule measurement is (leg, service type) | Helsinki–Pasila is run by both commuter and long-distance trains at different scheduled run times, so a per-leg padding figure blends two timetables — the same class of error as letting freight into the median. Splitting the unit also made the counterfactual self-consistent: the zero-strength replay now exactly reproduces measured carry-over, which the pooled version did not, and the focus line's headline gain fell from a flattering 27 points to a correct 19. |
| 2026-09-26 | Freeze scripts stream when the window can outgrow the pod | A year of raw rail JSON is 6.5 GB against ~6 GB of available RAM. The freeze reduces each day to compact accumulators on read (per-leg run times in `array('f')` with a parallel day index so any window can be sliced later) and takes a second pass over the files for the counterfactual replay rather than holding runs. Reading twice is cheaper than paging once. |
| 2026-09-26 | Seasonality separates volume from mechanism | The year was pulled to test whether a 60-day summer pack was hiding seasonality. It was hiding some: the day band widened from 67–90% to 56–94%. But the mechanism is near-constant — monthly late share swings 2.7–7.2% while carry-over stays inside 75–85% every month. Worth recording as a pattern: for this class of story, ask separately whether the *volume* and the *mechanism* are seasonal, because they need not be. |
| 2026-09-26 | The recovery-time mechanism is margin, not service type | Freezing 60 days turned the category contrast into something stronger: leg margin predicts delay survival at ρ **−0.96** across the seven lines, −0.85 across 68 legs, and −0.78 / −0.75 *within* commuter and long-distance separately. Negative-margin legs carry a delay across at 99%, 4+ min legs at 48%. Service type is only how margin is distributed today — and margin is the thing a timetable planner actually controls, so the claim became actionable instead of descriptive. A claim upgrade is proposed rather than taken, since the weaker one was the signed-off version. |
| 2026-09-26 | Recomputing on the real pack is a gate, not a formality | The 24-day verification concluded lines never separate (head-to-head a coin flip). On 60 days, 2 of 9 pairs separate robustly *and* the unifying explanation surfaced — separation tracks margin gap. Same finding, too small a sample. Everything a Spec quotes from verification is provisional until the pack exists; `docs/decision-specs/rail-recovery-time.md` carries the revision in the open rather than quietly restating. |
| 2026-09-26 | Redistribution has a floor, and it reframes the decision | The frozen counterfactual conserves each line's scheduled run time exactly (`journey_time_change_min` 0.00) and still takes long-distance carry-over from 77% to 50%. Commuter lines move 93% → 86% because they have nothing to redistribute. So for commuter the question is not *where* margin sits but whether to buy any, which costs journey time and is deliberately unmodelled. Found by running the counterfactual per line instead of only on the focus lane. |
| 2026-09-26 | A line is a modal route signature, never an average of stop positions | Averaging stop positions across trains that share two endpoints merged the Tampere and Savonia routes into a 38-station "Helsinki–Oulu" **no train runs** — the film would have drawn a line that does not exist. A line is the actual operated stop sequence, with expresses absorbed into the longest signature they are a subsequence of: 115 signatures → 34 real routes. Grouping by origin/destination is the opposite failure, starving all but one line. |
| 2026-09-26 | A reader control is verified like a figure | "Can the reader pick a line?" is two measurable claims: does each selectable line carry its own sample, and do lines differ. First is yes (7 routes, 6 inside a 60-day pack) and it sets the pack window. Second is **no** within a service type — head-to-head is a coin flip (7/16, 6/11 days), and the two comparisons that separate are the thinnest-sampled. So the picker ships as recognition, captions forbidden from implying a ranking. A control the evidence cannot support is as much a defect as a figure it cannot support. |
| 2026-09-26 | Category filters belong to every measurement, not the headline one | The rail pass filtered service category for delay survival but not for padding, so freight — timetabled far slower over the same rails — inflated scheduled run times and `YV→KOK` read 13.0 min of slack against 5.4 passenger-only. Corrected figures moved the story's claim from "the budget is in the wrong places" to the measured split: long-distance 2.1 min median padding / 3% negative legs vs commuter 0.2 min / 19%. Better claim, found only by recomputing. |
